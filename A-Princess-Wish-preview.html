✨ A Princess Wish

One shooting star. One heartfelt wish. One Christmas that changes everything.

Once upon a time, in a beautiful kingdom in Nigeria, there lived a princess with a heart full of love and one very special wish.

Every Christmas Eve, Princess Rosalina looks to the stars, hoping that one day she will find someone who will love her for who she truly is—not because she is a princess, but because of the person she is inside.

With Christmas approaching, Rosalina and her loyal best friend, Wendy, transform the castle into a magical wonderland filled with sparkling lights, delicious food, laughter, and festive surprises. But when a handsome young stranger arrives at the Christmas celebration, Rosalina begins to wonder whether her long-awaited wish might finally come true.

Prince David is charming, but it is what lies within his heart that may change Rosalina's life forever. Through unexpected surprises, acts of kindness, and a very special broken vase, the princess discovers that the greatest love is not about crowns or riches. It is about caring enough to mend what is broken, giving without expecting anything in return, and finding a family where love can flourish.

Filled with Christmas magic, Nigerian traditions, playful adventures, romance, and a joyful wedding celebration, A Princess Wish is a heartwarming fairy tale for children and families to enjoy together.

Because sometimes, the wish that comes true is even more wonderful than the one we imagined.

❄️ Chapter OneA Princess Wish

Once upon a time, in a beautiful kingdom in Nigeria, there lived a princess named Rosalina.

Princess Rosalina had a smile that could brighten a room, a laugh that made children giggle, and a heart that was always looking for someone to share it with.

Every year, on the night before Christmas, Rosalina had a special tradition.

She would slip quietly from the castle, walk into the garden, and sit beneath the twinkling stars.

And she would wait.

She was waiting for a shooting star.

Because Rosalina had a wish.

Tonight, she wrapped a soft shawl around her shoulders and settled beneath her favorite tree. The night air was warm, and the sounds of the village floated gently toward the castle.

She looked up.

One star.

Then another.

Then another.

“Please,” she whispered, “just one shooting star.”

She closed her eyes for a moment and imagined the person she hoped to meet someday.

Her soulmate.

In her imagination, he was handsome and strong, but gentle too. He made her laugh. He listened when she spoke. And when he stood beside her, Rosalina felt as though he had been there all her life.

She could almost feel his hand in hers.

Almost.

Then—

WHOOSH!

A bright streak of light raced across the sky.

Rosalina's eyes flew open.

“A shooting star!”

She quickly squeezed her eyes shut.

“I wish...” she whispered.

But what did she wish for?

Love.

Someone to share Christmas with.

Someone to laugh with.

Someone who would love her heart, not just her crown.

And most of all, someone who would make her feel that she was no longer alone.

Rosalina opened her eyes.

The shooting star had disappeared.

“Maybe this will be the year,” she whispered with a hopeful smile.

She hurried back toward the castle.

But when she opened the great wooden doors, her smile slowly faded.

The castle was quiet.

Too quiet.

Every year she decorated the castle herself.

Every year she hosted wonderful Christmas parties.

And every year, when Christmas morning arrived, she woke up alone.

She would open presents from her friends, the villagers, and people from the kingdom.

They were always kind.

But kindness wasn't quite the same as having someone special beside you.

Rosalina sighed.

“I wish Christmas didn't feel so lonely.”

She climbed the grand staircase toward her bedroom.

Rosalina was the only child of the kingdom's royal family. Her mother and father were gone now, and she missed them terribly.

The people of the kingdom loved their princess.

They wanted to see her happy.

They dreamed of the day she would find her prince, fall in love, and perhaps one day have children of her own.

But Rosalina didn't want to marry simply because everyone expected her to.

She wanted something more.

She wanted the kind of love she had imagined beneath the stars.

The kind of love that felt like coming home.

As Rosalina climbed into bed, she looked out her window one last time.

The moon shone brightly over the kingdom.

❄️ Chapter TwoTime to Start Decorating

The next morning, just as the sun was beginning to peek through the curtains, Princess Rosalina was fast asleep.

She was dreaming about Christmas trees, twinkling lights, and, of course, her handsome prince.

Suddenly—

“WAKE UP!”

Rosalina jumped.

“Ahhh!”

She sat straight up in bed.

Standing beside her was Wendy, her faithful servant and very best friend, with her hands on her hips and the biggest smile on her face.

“Come on! Come on! Wake up!” Wendy shouted. “We have lots to do today!”

Rosalina groaned and pulled the blankets over her head.

“Go away, Wendy.”

“No.”

“Please?”

“Nope.”

“Just five more minutes?”

“Absolutely not.”

Rosalina peeked out from underneath the blankets.

“Why not?”

“Because,” Wendy announced, “we are going to decorate this castle in style!”

Rosalina disappeared beneath the blankets again.

Wendy pulled them down.

“What will our theme be this year, my princess?”

“I don't care.”

“Wonderful!”

“I said I don't care.”

“Even better!”

Rosalina frowned.

“Wendy, I don't think you heard me.”

“Oh, I heard you perfectly.”

Rosalina pulled the blanket over her head again.

“I'll never find my prince.”

Wendy folded her arms.

“You're absolutely right.”

Rosalina froze.

Slowly, she lowered the blanket.

“You believe me?”

“Of course,” Wendy said.

Rosalina's face fell.

Wendy smiled.

“You're never going to find your prince while you're hiding under a blanket!”

Rosalina stared at her.

Then she burst out laughing.

“Wendy!”

“What?” Wendy asked innocently.

“You tricked me!”

“Perhaps.”

Rosalina grabbed the blanket and pulled it back over her head.

Wendy grabbed the other end.

“Oh no, you don't!”

“Give it back!”

“No!”

“Wendy!”

“Out of bed, Princess Rosalina!”

Wendy gave the blanket one big tug.

Rosalina came sliding right out of bed and landed on the floor with a soft thump!

For a moment, they both stared at each other.

Then Wendy started laughing.

Rosalina tried very hard not to laugh.

She failed.

Soon both of them were giggling.

“All right,” Rosalina said, climbing back onto her feet. “You win.”

“I always win.”

“You certainly think you do.”

“I know I do.”

Rosalina shook her head.

“You're impossible.”

“And you are hiding under blankets instead of helping me decorate.”

Wendy pointed toward the enormous Christmas tree waiting in the corner of the room.

“This year, we're going bigger than ever!”

Rosalina smiled.

“Really?”

“Really!”

“What do you have planned?”

Wendy's eyes sparkled.

“Crystalline hearts!”

Rosalina raised an eyebrow.

“And bells!”

“Of course.”

“And lights!”

“Lots of lights.”

“How many?”

Wendy thought for a moment.

“All of them.”

Rosalina laughed.

“All of them?”

“All of them!”

Wendy grabbed a notebook and began making a list.

“We'll have colorful lights throughout the castle. Red, white, pink, blue, gold—every color we can find!”

Rosalina's eyes grew wider.

“And holly?”

“Everywhere!”

“A giant Christmas tree?”

“Right in the center of the courtyard.”

Rosalina clapped her hands.

“I love it!”

“And the tree will have pink and red crystalline hearts, blue bells, silver decorations, and sparkling lights.”

Wendy paused dramatically.

“And…”

Rosalina leaned closer.

“What?”

“A giant gingerbread house!”

“In the courtyard?”

“In the courtyard!”

Rosalina burst out laughing.

“Wendy, a gingerbread house doesn't belong in a courtyard!”

“Why not?”

“The birds will eat it!”

Wendy thought about that.

“Oh.”

They both looked at each other.

Then Wendy grinned.

“Then we'll make it big enough for the birds to have their own Christmas party!”

Rosalina laughed so hard she had to sit down.

“All right,” she said. “Let's do it.”

Wendy held out her hand.

“Deal?”

Rosalina took it.

“Deal.”

Wendy pulled her toward the door.

“Come on!”

“Wait! I haven't even changed!”

“You're a princess. You can change while you're walking.”

“Wendy!”

“What?”

“You are definitely not allowed to be in charge of the castle.”

Wendy smiled.

“Too late.”

And with that, the two friends hurried down the hallway, ready to turn the quiet castle into the most magical Christmas kingdom anyone had ever seen.

❄️ Chapter ThreeMaking Christmas Cards and Invitations

Princess Rosalina had been looking everywhere for Wendy. She had checked the kitchen.

No Wendy.

The courtyard?

No Wendy.

The great Christmas tree?

No Wendy.

Even the pantry!

Still no Wendy.

“Where could she be?” Rosalina wondered.

There was only one place left to look.

The library.

Rosalina pushed open the enormous wooden doors.

The library was bright and beautiful. Tall windows stretched almost from the floor all the way to the ceiling, letting the warm morning sunlight pour across the room.

And there, standing beside one of the windows, was Wendy.

Rosalina stopped.

She blinked.

Then she blinked again.

“Wendy?”

Wendy didn't answer.

She was far too busy.

She had taped a sheet of paper to the window. On top of it, she had placed a snowflake stencil.

Then she picked up a stylus and carefully began tracing around the snowflake.

Rosalina watched for a few seconds.

“Wendy?”

“Yes?”

“What are you doing?”

“Making Christmas cards.”

“Ah.”

Wendy pressed harder with the stylus.

“Something isn't right.”

Rosalina smiled.

“What seems to be the problem?”

Wendy held up her card.

There wasn't much of a snowflake on it.

There was more of a strange collection of lines.

“Why isn't the snowflake showing up?”

Rosalina burst out laughing.

Wendy turned around.

“This isn't funny!”

“Oh, it is a little funny.”

Wendy put her hands on her hips.

“Well, if you're so clever, tell me what I'm doing wrong!”

Rosalina walked over and looked at the window.

“Well, for starters…”

Wendy waited.

“You taped the card stock to the window first.”

“Yes.”

“And then you put the stencil on top of it.”

“Yes.”

“And now you're trying to emboss the snowflake.”

“Yes!”

Rosalina pointed at the window.

“The stencil is supposed to be taped to the window first.”

Wendy stared at the stencil.

Then she stared at the card.

Then she stared at Rosalina.

“Why didn't you tell me that before?”

Rosalina giggled.

“Because I didn't know.”

Wendy's mouth dropped open.

“You didn't know?”

“No.”

“Then how did you know I was doing it wrong?”

“I remembered reading the instructions.”

Wendy sighed.

“Of course, you did.”

Rosalina looked down.

There were cards scattered all over the floor.

She picked one up.

Another strange-looking snowflake.

She picked up another.

Another strange-looking snowflake.

And another.

Rosalina looked at Wendy.

“How many cards have you made?”

Wendy looked away.

“That's not important.”

Rosalina smiled.

“How many?”

“Twenty-seven.”

“Twenty-seven?”

“Maybe thirty.”

Rosalina started laughing.

“Wendy!”

“Stop laughing!”

“I can't!”

Wendy crossed her arms.

“Fine. Laugh all you want.”

Then Rosalina noticed a large bag sitting on the table.

“What else have you got in there?”

Wendy's face suddenly brightened.

“Now you're going to see!”

She hurried over and began pulling out stencils.

“One Christmas tree!”

She held it up.

“One snowman!”

Another one.

“An angel!”

Another.

“A gingerbread house!”

Another.

“A gingerbread man!”

Another.

“A candy cane!”

Another.

“And finally…”

Wendy reached deep into the bag.

She pulled out one last stencil.

“The Bethlehem star!”

Rosalina looked at the collection.

“Is that everything?”

Wendy looked back into the bag.

She turned it upside down.

Nothing.

She shook it.

Still nothing.

She reached inside and searched again.

“Nope. That's all of them.”

Rosalina started laughing.

“Let me guess. You bought every Christmas stencil they had in the store!”

Wendy sighed.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“I wanted to try them all and see which one made the prettiest embossing for the Christmas cards and invitations.”

Rosalina smiled.

“Well, at least you came prepared.”

“Prepared?”

Wendy pointed at the mountain of failed cards.

“I came prepared to make a lot of mistakes.”

They both laughed.

Then Rosalina picked up the snowflake stencil.

“Why are you still using the snowflake?”

Wendy frowned.

“Because I haven't figured it out yet.”

“But you've already tried it how many times?”

Wendy counted on her fingers.

“Twenty-seven.”

Rosalina raised an eyebrow.

“Didn't you think it might be time to try another one?”

Wendy frowned.

“Go ahead. Have your little laugh.”

Rosalina giggled.

“Well, you have to admit, you've been trying the same thing over and over again.”

Wendy shook her head.

“You know, Princess, you weren't exactly perfect with the Christmas lights yesterday.”

Rosalina stopped laughing.

“What do you mean?”

Wendy smiled.

“Remember that one light bulb you kept putting into the Christmas lights?”

Rosalina thought for a moment.

“Oh…”

“The one that didn't work?”

Rosalina covered her face.

“Yes.”

“You kept taking it out and putting it back in.”

“I thought maybe it would work the next time.”

Wendy burst out laughing.

“And did it?”

“No.”

“Did I laugh at you?”

Rosalina shook her head.

“No.”

“What did I do?”

“You gave me another bulb.”

“And what happened?”

Rosalina smiled.

“The lights worked.”

“Exactly.”

Wendy picked up one of the failed cards and looked at it.

“So perhaps you shouldn't laugh too hard at me.”

Rosalina looked at the card in Wendy's hand.

Then she looked at Wendy.

“You're right.”

Wendy grinned.

“Besides, I didn't make fun of you because I knew you were getting frustrated.”

Rosalina smiled.

“That was very kind of you.”

“I know.”

Rosalina laughed.

“You really do think you're wonderful, don't you?”

“I don't think it. I know it.”

They both giggled.

Rosalina looked at the pile of crooked snowflakes on the table.

“I'm sorry, Wendy. I was teasing you when I should have been helping you.”

Wendy put her arm around Rosalina.

“And now you can help me.”

Rosalina smiled.

“Deal.”

“Good.”

Wendy handed her the snowflake stencil.

“Now, let's see if we can figure this thing out before I have to buy the entire paper shop.”

Rosalina laughed.

“That's probably a good idea.”

Together, they decided to try every stencil.

The Christmas tree looked pretty.

The snowman was cute.

The gingerbread man made Rosalina hungry.

The candy cane looked cheerful.

The angel was beautiful.

And the Bethlehem star was magnificent.

But when they finally tried the snowflake the right way, something wonderful happened.

The delicate snowflake appeared beautifully raised on the card.

Rosalina held it up to the sunlight.

“Oh, Wendy!”

Wendy smiled proudly.

“Now that is a Christmas card!”

They tried another one.

Then another.

And another.

Soon the library table was covered with beautiful embossed Christmas cards and invitations.

After much arguing, laughing, experimenting, and quite a few mistakes, they finally agreed.

The snowflake was their favorite.

“It looks elegant,” Rosalina said.

“And festive,” Wendy added.

“And it doesn't make me hungry,” Rosalina said.

Wendy laughed.

“You were thinking about eating the gingerbread man, weren't you?”

“Maybe.”

By the end of the afternoon, they had used almost all of their card stock.

Wendy looked at the empty pile.

“Oh dear.”

Rosalina looked at her.

“What?”

“We need more paper.”

Rosalina smiled.

“Back to the store?”

Wendy sighed.

“Back to the store.”

They looked at the floor covered with their practice cards.

Then they looked at each other.

And once again, they burst out laughing.

Christmas was going to be a lot of work.

But somehow, Rosalina thought it was already becoming the best Christmas she had ever had.
