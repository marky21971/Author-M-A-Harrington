A five-chapter preview

GOZEL

A story by M.A. Harrington

Where extraordinary horses meet the choices that shape a family.

Step into Turkmenistan during Nowruz, the Persian New Year, where tradition, food, family, and the breathtaking grace of the Akhal-Teke horse bring the landscape to life.

When Zara and her remarkable stallion, Gozel, win the festival’s horse-dance competition, a renowned breeder makes an offer that could help rescue her family’s struggling farm. But Gozel is more than a prize-winning horse—he is family, heritage, and a bond Zara cannot easily put a price on.

Then Gozel meets Jamila, the beautiful mare belonging to breeder Rizal, and the encounter surprises everyone. As the horses begin to connect, Zara and Rizal find themselves sharing more than a business arrangement. This is the beginning of a journey filled with heart, tradition, and choices whose consequences may reach far beyond the stable.

Discover the first five chapters of Gozel and experience Turkmenistan through the eyes of M.A. Harrington.

CHAPTER 1

Nowruz Festival

The Sumac Harvest The crisp morning air carried the scent of damp earth and budding life, a prelude to the vibrant celebration of Nowruz, the Persian New Year. Zara, her breath misting in the cool air, followed her mother through the rows of sumac bushes. The sun, just beginning its ascent, cast long shadows that danced and stretched across the landscape, illuminating the deep crimson clusters of berries hanging heavy on the branches. Each bush stood sentinel, its distinctive compound leaves, some with velvety twigs, a testament to the resilience of nature. The air hummed with the quiet industry of the morning, a symphony of rustling leaves and the chirping of birds, a soundtrack to their annual harvest. Their task was a sacred one: gathering sumac for the Haft-Seen table, the centerpiece of their Nowruz celebration. The berries, carefully picked and laid gently in woven baskets, would be dried slowly, a process requiring patience and care. The slow drying was crucial; it preserved the vibrant color and the tangy, almost lemony flavor that made sumac such a treasured ingredient in their cuisine.

Preparing the Haft-Seen Table As they worked, their hands moving with practiced grace, mother and daughter engaged in a conversation as familiar and comforting as the rhythm of their movements. They spoke of the preparations for the Haft-Seen, the seven symbolic items representing prosperity and good fortune. They discussed the arrangement of the table, its centerpiece a gleaming copper sahan, a family heirloom passed down through generations. The sahan, with its engraved Akhal-Teke horses— symbols of strength, grace, and family unity—held a special significance for Zara and her mother. The horses, galloping across the copper surface, mirrored the family’s own dynamic: a warm, affectionate, and fiercely supportive atmosphere where each member encouraged the others to strive for their best. The Haft-Seen table, they agreed, would be a vibrant tapestry of colors and textures. Purple and white crocus, their delicate petals unfurling in the gentle morning light, would be arranged alongside cheerful daffodils and tulips in a riot of colors. Samanu and Other Delicacies The traditional dishes, each holding its own symbolic meaning, would be carefully placed around the sahan: sabzeh (wheat or barley), representing rebirth and renewal; seer (garlic), symbolizing health and protection; sejed (dried olives), signifying peace and prosperity; serkeh (vinegar), representing patience and endurance; the dried sumac, their own contribution, representing the bounty of the earth; seeb (apple), a symbol of beauty and health; and samanu, the rich, velvety sweet pudding made from wheat germ, representing strength and the sweetness of life. Samanu, they both knew, was a labor of love. The slow cooking process, requiring hours of gentle simmering, allowed the natural sugars in the wheat germ to develop, creating a pudding with a deep, caramel-like flavor and a velvety texture that melted in the mouth. It’s warm, golden-brown color, often enhanced with the addition of nuts or dried fruits, added a touch of warmth and tradition to the Haft-Seen table. More than just a delicious treat, samanu represented the rewards of hard work and the blessings of the new year, a sweet culmination of the year’s efforts and a promise of abundance to come.

Zara’s Preparations

As the sun climbed higher, casting its warm glow over the landscape, Zara and her mother completed their task. Their baskets overflowed with the crimson berries, a testament to their morning’s work. The air, now warmer, carried the scent of the earth, the blossoms, and the anticipation of the festival. Their conversation shifted to the other preparations for Nowruz. Zara spoke of the barberries she would gather later in the day, the tart, ruby-red berries that would add a touch of brightness and a burst of acidity to the kuku sabzi, the vibrant green herb frittata that was a staple of their Nowruz celebration. Her mother, meanwhile, spoke of the sabzi polo mahi, the fragrant herb rice with fish, another culinary centerpiece of their Nowruz feast. The preparation of the sabzi polo mahi was a ritual in itself, a meticulous process that involved cooking the basmati rice with a generous mixture of finely chopped herbs, the saffron adding its signature golden hue and a delicate fragrance to the dish. The pan-fried fish, traditionally Caspian Kutum but often substituted with other white fish like tilapia or salmon, would be served alongside the herbed rice, a combination of flavors and textures that celebrated the abundance of the season.

Anticipation and Hope This year, however, there was an added layer of excitement. Zara, a skilled horsewoman, had been working tirelessly with her horse, Gozel, preparing for the Nowruz festival’s horse-riding competition. They had been practicing their dance routine, a graceful and intricate performance that required both skill and perfect synchronization. They had spent countless hours honing their movements, perfecting their timing, ensuring that their performance would be flawless. The grand prize, a substantial sum of money, was a much-needed financial boost for their family, and Zara and Gozel were hopeful for a win.

The thought of the prize money, which would alleviate some of the family’s financial burdens, added an extra layer of anticipation to their preparations for the festival. As the sun reached its zenith, casting its warm glow over the landscape, Zara and her mother returned home, their baskets filled with the bounty of their harvest. The air was alive with the sounds of the approaching festival, a symphony of laughter, music, and the joyful anticipation of a new beginning. The scent of the sumac, the barberries, and the promise of the upcoming feast filled the air, a fragrant reminder of the traditions and the bonds that held their family together. The Haft- Seen table, soon to be laden with its symbolic offerings, would be more than just a festive display; it would be a testament to their shared history, their enduring traditions, and their unwavering hope for a prosperous and joyful new year. The Nowruz festival was more than just a celebration; it was a renewal, a reaffirmation of their family’s values, and a promise of a brighter future.

CHAPTER 2

A Symphony of Hooves and Hearts

The city blazed with the incandescent joy of Nowruz. A kaleidoscope of color exploded before Zara and Gozel as they entered, the streets a vibrant tapestry woven with blossoming jasmine, vibrant banners snapping in the warm breeze, and the joyous cacophony of a thousand celebrations. The air itself hummed with the energy of renewal, a palpable sense of rebirth that tickled the senses. The aroma of grilling kebabs mingled with the sweet perfume of countless flowers, creating a heady fragrance that promised a day of unforgettable experiences. Even the sun seemed to shine brighter, illuminating the festive spirit that painted every building in a golden glow. This is it, Zara thought, a thrill of anticipation mixing with a familiar flutter of nerves, the moment of truth. Their destination: the heart-stopping spectacle of the annual horse dance competition. The square pulsed with anticipation, a sea of faces eager to witness the magic. Zara, perched atop Gozel, felt the familiar flutter of nerves, a thrill that mingled with the excitement. Gozel, sensing her anxiety, nuzzled her gently, his velvety nose a comforting presence against her cheek. His coat, a rich, deep chestnut, shimmered like polished mahogany under the sun, reflecting the kaleidoscope of colors around them. He's ready, she murmured, stroking his neck, and so am I. But a knot of apprehension tightened in her stomach. What if we don’t win? What if he falters?

As they approached the competition area, a hush fell over the crowd, a pregnant silence that held its breath before erupting into a thunderous roar of cheers. The spectators, many familiar faces who had witnessed Gozel’s breathtaking performances in years past, knew what to expect. Their anticipation was a tangible force, a wave of excitement that washed over Zara and Gozel, carrying them on its crest. Whispers and hushed comments about Gozel’s unparalleled grace and power had preceded them, fueling the fervent expectation. They expect a miracle, Zara thought, a wry smile playing on her lips, and we intend to deliver. The music began, a vibrant pulse that resonated deep within Zara’s soul. The crowd fell silent, their collective breath held captive by the anticipation. Then, Gozel transformed. He wasn’t merely dancing - he was weaving a spell, a breathtaking ballet of power and elegance. Each movement was a masterpiece of fluid grace, a symphony of controlled energy. His mane, a dark river of silk, flowed in time with the music, his eyes gleaming with an almost otherworldly intelligence. He’s magnificent, Zara thought, her heart swelling with pride, absolutely magnificent. It wasn’t just skill; it was art, raw emotion poured into motion; the very spirit of Nowruz given form. The crowd watched, transfixed, as Gozel’s dance unfolded – a story whispered in the language of movement, a tale of spring’s triumphant return. This is what we live for, she thought, this connection, this shared moment of magic. Unseen in the crowd, Rizal, a horse breeder of legendary renown, watched Gozel’s performance with a heart that hammered against his ribs. He had witnessed countless equine displays, but nothing prepared him for the sheer artistry of Gozel’s dance. The breathtaking power and grace, the effortless elegance – it was an enchantment. He’s perfect, Rizal thought, his mind already racing, the ideal mate for Jamila. He knew, with a certainty that resonated deep within his soul, that he had to possess Gozel. Not merely to admire, but to secure his bloodline, to weave it into the tapestry of his own legacy.

As the music faded and the thunderous applause shook the very ground beneath their feet, Rizal approached Zara. “Congratulations on a truly magnificent victory,” he boomed, his voice rich with genuine admiration. “My name is Rizal, and your horse… your horse is nothing short of extraordinary. I wonder… would you consider breeding Gozel with my mare, Jamila?” Zara’s initial response was a sharp, instinctive “No!” The idea of Gozel, her cherished companion, her partner in this dance of life, being bred with another horse felt like a violation, a betrayal of their unique bond. No, absolutely not, she thought fiercely, he’s more than just a prize-winning horse, he’s family. But the weight of her family’s precarious financial situation pressed heavily upon her, a stark reality that overshadowed her initial objections. The prize money, while substantial, was a fleeting solution to their persistent struggles. But we need the money, she thought, a wave of despair washing over her, we need it desperately. Rizal’s offer represented a lifeline, a chance to secure their future, to alleviate the constant worry that gnawed at their hearts. It’s a terrible choice, she thought, but what other choice do we have? Her voice softened, her resolve wavering under the weight of necessity. “Let me discuss this with my family,” she said, her tone betraying the turmoil within. “We will speak again soon.” I need time, she thought, time to think, to pray, to find a way to make the right decision. Rizal, understanding her hesitation, presented his business card. “You can reach me here,” he said, his voice conveying respect for her decision. “Tomorrow,” Zara replied, her voice barely a whisper, a mixture of hope and trepidation swirling within her. Tomorrow, she thought, tomorrow will bring its own challenges.

The ride home was a whirlwind of conflicting emotions, a symphony of hope and despair playing out within her heart. The family home was already alive with the vibrant energy of the celebration. Laughter, the clinking of glasses, and the joyous chatter of family and friends filled the air as Zara entered, a stark contrast to the turmoil in her heart. She shared the news of Gozel’s victory, her words met with joyous exclamations that echoed through the house. But when she revealed Rizal’s proposition, a sudden hush fell over the room, the festive atmosphere momentarily stilled. Now what? She thought, bracing herself for the difficult conversation ahead. Her father, a man deeply rooted in tradition and the preservation of his family’s heritage, broke the silence. “Let us continue this celebration,” he said, his voice a calming balm amidst the storm brewing within Zara’s heart. “We shall address Gozel’s future tomorrow. For now, let us savor this moment of joy, and let us remain positive.” His words, though intended to soothe, did little to dispel the weight on Zara’s heart. He’ll never agree, she thought, but how can I refuse? He vehemently opposed the idea of breeding Gozel, fearing the irreversible dilution of his unique bloodline, a legacy he held sacred. Yet, the harsh reality of their financial struggles remained, a constant reminder of their precarious situation. The decision loomed, a difficult choice between preserving heritage and securing their immediate future, a choice that would shape the destiny of Gozel and their family for generations to come. Oh, Gozel, she thought, stroking his flank, what will become of us?

CHAPTER 3

The Stallion’s Price

The jeep rattled along the dusty road, each jarring bump a physical echo of the turmoil tearing through Zara’s father. His knuckles, bone-white against the worn steering wheel, mirrored the clenching anxiety in his chest. Rizal’s proposal—to breed Gozel, their prize Akhal-Teke stallion—hung over them like a suffocating weight, a shadow cast by their desperate financial situation. He stole a glance at Zara, her face etched with a worry that mirrored his own. The unspoken fear hung heavy between them, a palpable tension woven from debt and the looming threat of losing everything. “This Rizal…” he rasped, his voice a rough whisper against the rattling of the vehicle, “He’s a wealthy man. He doesn’t understand… us.” The words were a choked confession of their vulnerability, a raw admission of their desperation. Zara leaned closer, her own voice trembling slightly. “Papa, he loves Jamila. You should have heard him speak of her—the tenderness in his voice, the light in his eyes… it was… different.” Her voice cracked, the unshed tears a testament to the emotional toll this decision was taking. Her father’s sigh was a mournful sound, a weary exhale that carried the weight of years of struggle. “Love doesn’t pay the bills, Zara. We’re drowning in debt. The bank… they’re threatening foreclosure. This breeding… it’s a lifeline. But…” his voice caught, choked by the bitter truth, “Selling a piece of our soul for a few coins… it feels like betraying everything we stand for.” The silence that followed was a suffocating blanket, woven from unspoken fears and the crushing weight of their precarious situation. The journey felt endless, each mile stretching into an eternity of worry.

Rizal’s hacienda, when they finally arrived, was a jarring contrast to their humble farm—a stark reminder of their desperate circumstances. The opulence felt like a cruel mockery, a painful display of wealth that underscored their own poverty. Rizal’s forced cheerfulness felt hollow, a thin veneer over the underlying tension. He spoke of the breeding, of the superior genetics, the potential for prize-winning foals, the financial rewards, but his words were devoid of the passion Zara had witnessed. His clinical description of Jamila felt like a betrayal of the deep affection she knew he held for the mare. “This breeding will be mutually beneficial,” Rizal stated, his voice confident, yet lacking the warmth Zara remembered. “Gozel’s pedigree, combined with Jamila’s… the offspring will command a high price.” The words, though technically accurate, felt cold, devoid of the emotional connection Zara felt towards their stallion. Zara’s father’s voice was tight, strained with suppressed desperation. “Your offer… it’s not enough, Rizal. We need more. We need to secure our farm’s future. Our family’s future.” The words were a plea, a desperate cry for survival. Rizal frowned, his confidence faltering slightly. “I offered a generous sum, more than fair considering Gozel’s pedigree. I’m surprised by your hesitation.” “Hesitation?” Zara’s father’s voice rose, the suppressed desperation finally erupting. “This isn’t just about money, Rizal! Gozel is our heritage, our pride. He’s more than just a stallion! And you, sir, spoke of Jamila as if she were a mere commodity, not the cherished companion Zara described.” His voice cracked, the raw emotion pouring forth. The argument threatened to consume them, a painful stalemate looming. Then, Zara intervened, her voice trembling but resolute, a lifeline thrown across the chasm of conflict. “Papa,” she said, her gaze moving between her father and Rizal, her voice thick with unshed tears, “what if… what if Jamila comes to our farm? I can care for both Gozel and Jamila. It would ease some of Papa’s concerns, wouldn’t it?”

Her father stared at her, his expression softening, a glimmer of hope piercing the despair. The suggestion, born from necessity and a deep understanding of both men’s hearts, offered a compromise he hadn’t considered. Rizal, his eyes widening slightly, considered the proposal. The unexpected solution addressed both the financial and emotional aspects of the deal. He saw the desperation, the raw vulnerability in Zara’s father’s eyes, the genuine concern for their animals. He saw the love in Zara’s eyes, a love that extended to both her family and his mare. Losing this deal was unthinkable. “I agree,” Rizal said, his voice surprisingly soft, a hint of relief in the quiet tone. “Jamila will come to your farm. And… I will pay you for your help in caring for both horses.” He paused, his gaze locking with Zara’s, a subtle shift in his demeanor, a vulnerability he hadn’t shown before. “But… on one condition.” Zara’s father held his breath, bracing himself. But Rizal’s next words were unexpected, a surprising twist that shifted the dynamics of the situation. “I will come and live with you,” Rizal stated, his gaze unwavering. “I cannot bear to be separated from Jamila during this crucial period.” Zara’s father stared at him, stunned. He had assumed Rizal was solely driven by profit. This unexpected offer, this willingness to integrate himself into their lives, shifted his perception. He saw a genuine concern for the horses, A willingness to go beyond mere financial transactions. A glimmer of hope pierced the despair. Zara, too, was taken aback. This was a significant step, a leap of faith. The silence that followed was filled with a different kind of tension, a mixture of surprise, uncertainty, and a fragile hope. Zara’s father looked from Zara to Rizal, then back again. He weighed the risks and the rewards, the financial stability against the intrusion into their lives. But the unexpected turn of events, Rizal’s genuine concern for the wellbeing of his mare, had changed the dynamics of the situation. The previously insurmountable obstacles seemed suddenly surmountable.

CHAPTER 4

The Unexpected

The next day dawned bright, bringing with it the arrival of Rizal and Jamila. Zara’s heart pounded a nervous rhythm against her ribs. This was it – the moment of truth. She led Gozel, her handsome stallion, to the fenced-in pasture, securing him safely within before guiding Jamila from the horse trailer. Rizal, ever gentle, held the lead rope, his brow furrowed with a mixture of hope and apprehension. The air crackled with unspoken tension. As Rizal led Jamila from the trailer, she initially appeared calm, her gait steady and measured. But the moment Jamila caught the faintest whiff of Gozel, a transformation occurred. Her ears shot forward, her nostrils flared, and a low rumble vibrated in her chest. The rumble escalated into a sharp, piercing whinny that sliced through the quiet morning air. Her head snapped up, and with a sudden, powerful surge, she reared, her front legs leaving the ground. Rizal, caught off guard by the unexpected display of energy, stumbled backward, his grip on the lead rope weakening. The rope slipped from his grasp, and Jamila, now completely free, broke into a powerful gallop toward Gozel, her mane flying in the wind. Rizal, regaining his balance, let out a cry of alarm. “Jamila, no!” he shouted, his voice filled with a mixture of fear and desperation. Zara’s breath hitched. Oh no, oh God no, she thought, her heart leaping into her throat. This is not how it was supposed to go. Please, let them be okay. The frantic pounding of her own heart mirrored the thundering hooves of Jamila. This is all my fault. I should have anticipated this. I should have done something different. The fear was a cold fist clenching around her chest, squeezing the air from her lungs. Gozel! Please, Gozel, be safe. She reacted instantly, sprinting alongside Rizal, their hearts pounding in unison with every frantic step. The scene unfolded before them with terrifying speed, a blur of motion and sound.

Zara’s eyes were wide, her face a mask of worry. Every muscle in her body screamed with a primal fear for both horses. They raced across the pasture, the distance between them and the two horses seemingly insurmountable. The wind whipped through their hair, carrying with it the sounds of Jamila’s thundering hooves and the frantic beating of their own hearts. The world narrowed to the urgent need to reach Jamila and prevent a potential catastrophe. As they drew closer, however, a strange thing happened. The anticipated collision, the anticipated chaos, failed to materialize. Instead, the two horses met in a moment of unexpected tranquility. Jamila, her initial wild energy Seemingly spent, lowered her head, and Gozel, equally surprised, mirrored her action. Their muzzles touched, a tentative, hesitant contact that spoke volumes of unspoken understanding. Rizal and Zara skidded to a halt, their hearts still racing, but their fear slowly replaced by a wave of incredulous relief. The air, moments ago thick with the palpable tension of impending disaster, now hummed with a surprising calm. Instead of the anticipated clash, a serene connection seemed to be forming between the two magnificent animals. They watched, transfixed, as Jamila and Gozel stood nose to nose, a silent conversation passing between them, a language only horses could understand. Zara let out a shaky breath, the tension slowly ebbing away. They’re alright… they’re actually… getting along? Rizal, his breath finally returning to a normal rhythm, looked at Zara, a slow smile spreading across his face. The tension that had gripped him for the past few minutes began to melt away, replaced by a sense of awe and wonder. He had expected conflict, but instead, he witnessed a blossoming connection. “Looks like Jamila loved the scent and look of Gozel,” Rizal said, his voice still slightly shaky. Zara, her own relief palpable, laughed, wiping a tear from her eye.

The worry, the anxiousness, the fear – it all seemed to melt away like morning mist. “Yeah,” she replied, her voice filled with emotion. “And Gozel likes Jamila too. It’s… unexpected.” “What does ‘Jamila’ mean?” Zara asked, her gaze softening as she looked at the two horses now nuzzling each other gently. Rizal blushed, a deep crimson rising in his cheeks. His love for Jamila was profound, a deep and abiding affection that transcended words. “It means beautiful,” he whispered, his voice thick with emotion. Zara smiled, a gentle, knowing smile that reflected her own deep understanding. “Gozel means beautiful, too,” she said softly. “Maybe… maybe this is destiny unfolding.” She paused, then added with a chuckle, “Or maybe they just really like each other’s style.” A moment of silence hung between them, broken only by the soft sounds of the two horses interacting. The sun warmed their faces, and the air felt lighter, cleaner. The fear and anxiety had been replaced by a sense of peace and wonder. They decided to open the gate, letting the two horses into the larger pasture to play, to get to know each other, to bond in their own unique way. The unexpected had happened, and it was beautiful.

CHAPTER 5

Playful Day

Rizal, his heart brimming with quiet joy, watched Jamila and Gozel. The unfamiliar, tantalizing smells from the house—baking bread and simmering spices—reached even the stables, stirring his appetite. It promised a culinary adventure. Just as he reached for the stable door, Zara appeared, her face radiant. “Come on,” she said, her voice bright, “Let’s eat first, then we can tend to the horses.” Rizal followed her, the enticing smells intensifying. This was his first true taste of Turkistan cuisine, and a wave of anticipation washed over him. The meal was a revelation. The rich, complex flavors were astounding. Zara's father, a jovial man with twinkling eyes, joined them. "So, you're the horse expert Zara's been raving about," he boomed, clapping Rizal on the back. "We're lucky to have you. This project with Gozel is a big undertaking – a year and a half, at least, from pregnancy to weaning. That's a considerable commitment, wouldn't you say?" Rizal chuckled. "A year and a half is indeed a significant commitment, sir. But it's a privilege to work with such magnificent animals, and your hospitality makes the long days more enjoyable." He paused, considering. "It will be a rewarding experience to witness the foal's development." The father chuckled. "Ah, you've got a good eye, young man! Zara's cooking is legendary, but don't let that fool you, these Akhal-Tekes require patience, understanding… and a bit of luck. I trust you'll be providing plenty of all three." He winked. "And maybe a little charm to keep my daughter happy." After breakfast, they returned to the stables. They led Jamila and Gozel to the open, fenced-in pasture and released them.

The moment they were free, Jamila, with a joyful snort, launched into a series of exuberant bucks, her mane flying, her powerful legs propelling her high into the air. “Whee!” she thought, a silent, joyful exclamation echoing in her mind. “This is amazing! I feel so free!” Gozel, equally enthusiastic, responded with a playful chase, his sleek coat gleaming in the sunlight as he galloped after her, his whinnies echoing across the pasture. “Catch me if you can!” he thought, his powerful legs carrying him effortlessly across the field. “She’s fast, but I’m faster!” They chased each other in a dizzying dance of playful pursuit, their movements fluid and graceful. They intertwined necks, playfully nipped at each other’s manes, and rolled in the grass, kicking up clouds of dust in their joyous abandon. Later, after a long, happy playtime, Jamila rested her head on the grass. "That was the best run ever!" she thought contentedly. "I feel so much better now. I hope we get to play again tomorrow." Gozel, nuzzling the grass, murmured internally, "She's fast, but I'm faster... next time, I'll win!" Rizal leaned against the gate, watching Zara. As she climbed the fence, a sudden, unexpected wave of feeling washed over, him. He found himself captivated by her grace, her easy confidence, the way the sunlight caught the strands of her hair. “She’s… incredibly beautiful,” he thought, the realization striking him with surprising force. But as quickly as the feeling arose, he pushed it down. “Professionalism,” he reminded himself sternly. “Keep it professional. This is a business arrangement, nothing more.” He watched Zara settle onto the fence, his own smile a carefully constructed mask over a heart that was beating a little faster than it should. Zara settled onto the fence, watching the horses. “They’re so happy,” she thought, a warm feeling spreading through her chest. “It’s good to see them like this, free and playful. And Rizal… he seems content, too. He’s good with the horses; I can see that. But… there’s something about him. Something quiet, something intense… I wonder what he’s thinking. He seems so serious, so focused on his work… but I saw that look in his eyes just now, when he watched me climb the fence.

A flicker of something… I can’t quite place it. Maybe it’s just the sun, or maybe… maybe there’s something more there. But I shouldn’t think about that. We’re partners, that’s all. Just partners. For now.” She smiled. The sun sank slowly, leaving behind a sky streaked with fading light. A stillness settled over the pasture, broken only by the occasional rustle of the wind through the tall grass. The day's energy had dissipated, leaving behind a quiet expectancy, a sense of anticipation for the unfolding of tomorrow.
