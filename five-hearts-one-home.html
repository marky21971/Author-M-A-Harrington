Five Hearts, One Home

By M. A. Harrington

But Five Hearts, One Home became more than a story I wrote for my mom.

It became a story about love.

We often grow up believing that love is supposed to fit neatly into a box. Society has created ideas about what a relationship should look like, how many people should be involved, and what love is supposed to mean. But love has never been quite that simple.

There are many ways to love, and there are many ways for people to build a life together.

This story explores one of those possibilities.

I wanted to show that people in LGBTQ+ relationships are not fundamentally different from people in heterosexual relationships. Beneath the labels, we all share many of the same basic human needs. We want to be loved. We want to feel desired, respected, safe, valued, and understood. We want someone to listen when we're hurting, hold us when we're afraid, celebrate with us when we're happy, and choose us—not because we are perfect, but because we are ourselves.

We may express love differently. We may build relationships differently. We may choose lives that don't look like everyone else's.

But that doesn't make the need for love any less universal.

Five Hearts, One Home isn't meant to suggest that one way of loving is better than another. It is simply an invitation to look beyond the boxes society sometimes creates for us and remember that, at the heart of every relationship, there are human beings searching for connection.

And perhaps that is what we all have in common.

We all want somewhere we can belong.

Somewhere we can be ourselves.

Somewhere we can love and be loved.

Somewhere we can call home.
